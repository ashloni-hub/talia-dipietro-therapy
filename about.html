the approach

Therapy Is a Tool, Not a Dependency

Many of the people who contact me carry a worry they rarely say out loud. They think starting therapy means signing up for something they will never finish. That is not how I practice. The goal is to give you enough understanding of yourself, and enough skill, that you stop needing me.

The work is active. We look at what is driving a pattern, name it plainly, and build something you can do differently. Sometimes that means rehearsing a hard conversation before you have it. Sometimes it is practical coping for a day that is already going badly. Sometimes it is working out what self-care means for you specifically, rather than what it means on the internet.

People often arrive here after therapy that consisted of telling the story and nothing more. Understanding what happened matters. It is not the whole job. I will name what I see, and I will ask you to try things between sessions.

I see clients in person at my Larchmont office, serving Westchester County and the Bronx, and online across New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania. I am currently accepting new clients.

meet talia

Meet Talia DiPietro

I became an LMSW in 2010, an LCSW in 2014, and I have been in private practice since 2014. My narcissistic abuse recovery training is a Certified Narcissistic Abuse Treatment Clinician (NATC) certification with Dr. Ramani Durvasula. I am trained in AEDP Level 1, which informs the way I practice.

The core of my work sits in attachment-oriented and relational therapy, with CBT and DBT skills brought in where they earn their place.

I work with people in a wide range of life and relationship situations, including people in non-monogamous relationships, people in sex work, and people in the kink community. Very little surprises me. That matters more than it sounds, because the thing you are dreading having to explain is usually the thing worth talking about.

How I Work

Skills, Not Just Story

Insight on its own does not change how next week goes. Every stretch of work has a practical end: a skill to practice, a conversation to rehearse, a boundary to try out.

Nothing Here Is Off Limits

Therapy With an Exit
