Why Parenting Brings Your Own Childhood Back

Parenting puts you back inside a childhood you may have spent decades getting clear of. A tone of voice. A particular age. A specific kind of defiance, and something old arrives well before you have decided anything about it.

The trigger is usually specific rather than general. Many parents do fine until their child reaches the age they were when things went wrong, or until a situation echoes one they were on the other side of. Then the reaction outruns the moment.

None of this means you are repeating what was done to you. Noticing the pull is the thing that separates you from it, and the parents who find a page like this one are usually already doing the part that matters most.

how it works

How I Approach This Work

We find the specific trigger. Parenting itself is rarely the issue. It is usually one age, one behavior, or one particular tone.

Skills for ten seconds. Most of what you end up regretting happens in the gap between the trigger and your response, so we build something you can use inside that gap.

Repair matters more than perfection. You will lose it sometimes. Rupture and repair is well described in attachment work, and repairing afterward is a skill we can practise here directly.

Shame gets addressed head on. Parents carry shame that stops them saying the thing out loud. Whatever it is, it can be said here, and saying it out loud is usually where the work starts.

Your own history gets room. This is not parenting coaching with a trauma label on it. What happened to you gets worked on in its own right, because that is what drives the reaction.

What I Help With

Losing your temper. Work on the anger that arrives faster and bigger than the moment standing in front of you ever called for.

Shutting down and going cold. Support when your response to being overwhelmed is to disappear rather than to raise your voice at anybody at all.

One age that is harder. Work on the particular stage of your child’s life that lands differently because of what happened at that age for you.

The shame afterward. Help with the shame that follows a bad moment and is almost always far out of scale with the moment itself.

Fear of repeating it. Work on the fear that what was done to you is going to travel down to them, and on what makes that less likely.

Your parent as a grandparent. Support when your own parent is now in your child’s life and you are deciding what access should look like.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my own child trigger me so much?

Usually because something in the moment matches something old: a tone, an age, a kind of defiance, or a situation you were once on the other side of. The response fires before you have processed anything, which is why it feels like it comes from nowhere.

Why do I get so angry at my kids over small things?

When a small thing produces a large reaction, the size of the reaction is generally about something other than the small thing. Anger that arrives faster and bigger than the situation warrants is one of the most common ways an old stress response shows up.

Is it normal to be triggered by your own child?

It is extremely common among parents who had difficult childhoods, and it is talked about far less than it happens. Being triggered is not evidence that you are a bad parent. It is evidence that parenting is putting you near something unresolved.

How do I stop repeating my parents’ patterns?

Mostly by getting specific: which pattern, in which situation, at which age. Broad resolutions rarely survive a hard evening, whereas knowing your particular trigger and having something to do in that moment is workable. Repair afterward does real work too.

Is this the same as trauma-informed parenting?

Not quite, and the distinction matters when you are searching. Trauma-informed parenting usually means parenting a child who has been through something difficult, and most of the material online is written for foster and adoptive families. This page is about your own history.
